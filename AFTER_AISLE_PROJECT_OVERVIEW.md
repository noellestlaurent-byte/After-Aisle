# After Aisle — Business Overview for Claude Projects

## What It Is
After Aisle is a curated, vetted wedding decor resale marketplace — the "national gallery for the high-standard bride." It solves a gap in the wedding industry: brides currently have two options for decor — pay thousands to rent items for 24 hours, or scour chaotic, unvetted resale platforms like Facebook Marketplace. After Aisle is the third option: a quality-controlled, aesthetic-matched circular marketplace where brides can buy, use, and resell decor — recouping their investment without sacrificing the look they envisioned.

Make high-quality, editorial wedding decor accessible to the intentional bride — and give that decor a life beyond one day.

A circular decor economy built on curation, trust, and aesthetic standards. Not a dumping ground. A gallery.

## Target Customer (ICP)
Brides/grooms aged 25–32 planning a wedding that is aesthetic, high-quality, and social-media-worthy — on a moderate budget. They are:
- Social-media-savvy and trend-aware
- Budget-conscious but not cheap — they have a clear aesthetic vision and won't compromise it
- Frustrated by the binary of "rent expensive" or "buy chaotic"
- Likely discovered After Aisle through TikTok

---

## Hypothesis
"Financially savvy brides with a clear, social-media-inspired aesthetic vision are forced to choose between money (paying thousands to rent decor for just 24 hours) or time (scouring chaotic, unvetted resale marketplaces) — and will pay a curation fee for a third option: a vetted, aesthetic-matched marketplace where they can buy, use, and resell decor, recouping their investment without sacrificing the look they've envisioned."

---

## Business Model
- **Type:** Two-sided marketplace (buyers and sellers)
- **Revenue:** 20% commission on every sale — no subscription fees for buyers or sellers
- **Founding seller incentive:** First 25 founding sellers pay 0% commission forever

---

## Operational Plan

**Beta Strategy — Seller-First**
Build supply before demand. Founding sellers are vetted, incentivized, and onboarded with white-glove attention. Target: 25 founding sellers with high-quality inventory before opening to buyers.

**Founding Seller Criteria:**
- Items meet listing standards (condition, price floor, safety)
- Wedding has passed or is within 60 days
- Seller understands and agrees to shipping/packaging standards
- Items have resale value of $30+

**Founding Seller Incentives:**
- 0% commission forever (vs. 20% standard)
- First listings featured prominently at launch
- "Founding Seller" badge on profile
- Direct line to Noelle for onboarding support

**Timeline:** Targeting June/July 2026 launch

---

## Listing Standards (Objective Rejection Criteria)
Items are rejected based on functional standards only — not aesthetic taste:
- Listed below $50 minimum
- Permanent staining or visible damage
- Structural damage (broken, cracked, missing parts)
- Mold, mildew, or unremediable odor
- Rust or corrosion
- Burned candles
- Unsanitizable porous fabric
- Safety hazards (exposed wiring, broken glass, unstable bases)
- Consumables (fresh/dried florals, food, single-use items)
- Items the seller does not own outright

---

## Seller Onboarding & Shipping Standards
Full guide lives at afteraisle.com/seller-guide. Key policies:
- **Condition tiers:** Like New / Gently Used / Used — Good / Used — Fair
- **Photo requirements:** Full item shot, detail shot, any wear/imperfection, professional wedding photo, timestamped photo
- **48-hour buyer inspection window** — funds auto-release from escrow if no dispute filed
- **Packaging standards** by item type (arch frames flagged as best for local pickup)
- **Dispute & damage policy** based on research of Poshmark, Depop, The RealReal, eBay, Pickle, StockX

## Competitors
- **Wedzee** — wedding resale marketplace, low activity, appears largely inactive
- **Wedlender** — rental-focused, early stage (6 followers, no posts as of research), still in testing
- **Facebook Marketplace / Craigslist** — chaotic, unvetted, the status quo After Aisle replaces
- **Rental companies** — high cost ($2,800+ for arches for 24 hrs), no ownership
- **The RealReal / Poshmark / Depop** — general resale, no wedding-specific curation or vetting

**Competitive advantage:** The only platform combining vetting, aesthetic curation, and a circular ownership model specifically for wedding decor. Competitors are either too expensive, too chaotic, or too inactive.

---

## Key Risks / Things to Watch
- Logistics and shipping damage affecting brand reputation
- Quality control at scale — maintaining editorial standards as inventory grows
- Timing mismatch between seller wedding date and platform launch
- Building demand side (buyers) after supply is established
- Commission model sustainability vs. founding seller 0% promise long-term
